\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}
This chapter summarises the contributions of this dissertation, states its findings against the research gap identified in Chapter~\ref{ch:litreview}, and sets out the future work that follows directly from the limitations established in Chapter~\ref{chap:results_and_discussion}.

\section{Summary of Contributions}
This dissertation proposed and evaluated a dual-path methodology for detecting dynamic, temporally dependent web UI defects, such as flickering, layout shifts, and broken animations, that escape both the conventional pixel-level visual regression oracle and naive frame-uniform application of Vision-Language Models. The methodology combines automated, reproducible fault injection into recorded interaction video (Section~\ref{sec:data_prep}); a choice of frame-selection strategies that route pixel and DOM-mutation evidence to a zero-shot VLM judge (Section~\ref{sec:system_architecture}); and a pre-registered, power-analysed factorial ablation that tests the pipeline's own design choices against one another, rather than reporting a single end-to-end configuration as if it were self-evidently correct (Section~\ref{sec:exp_design}).

Three contributions follow from this work. The first is an automated, seed-reproducible dataset of 120 interaction recordings spanning six categories of temporal UI defect, validated by two independent automated quality gates, one of which was added specifically in response to a discovered dataset-integrity failure mode (Section~\ref{sec:data_prep-generation}), so that the defects reported as present are provably present on the recorded video and not merely in a paired screenshot. The second is a frame-selection and prompting architecture, together with an explicit, code-level demonstration (Section~\ref{sec:label_leak}) that the injected ground-truth label cannot reach the judge model, which the empirical evaluation in Chapter~\ref{chap:results_and_discussion} shows raises detection accuracy from \SI{75.3}{\percent} (uniform sampling, naive prompt) to \SI{90.8}{\percent} (pixel-only keyframe selection, dual-path prompt) while eliminating false positives on normal clips entirely, at lower token cost than the baseline. The third is a negative result, arrived at through pre-registered ablation rather than avoided: fusing DOM-mutation evidence into frame \emph{selection} was hypothesised to improve on pixel-only selection, but the formal trial found it added no measurable accuracy under a fixed prompt and actively reduced accuracy once combined with the dual-path prompt it was designed to pair with (Findings 1 and 2, Section~\ref{sec:results-findings}). This dissertation reports that reversal explicitly, and reframes pixel-only selection with the dual-path prompt (A1B1), rather than the originally intended fused-selection configuration (A3B1), as its proposed method (Section~\ref{sec:results-decisions}).

\section{Answering the Research Gap}
Chapter~\ref{ch:litreview} identified a gap at the intersection of three observations: pixel-level visual regression testing operates on static screenshot pairs and cannot represent defects whose evidence exists only across time; off-the-shelf VLMs applied to such defects oscillate between excessive sensitivity and excessive conservatism rather than resolving the pixel oracle's false-positive problem; and no existing work combines selective, evidence-driven video input construction with structural (DOM) evidence for this specific class of defect. The evaluation in Chapter~\ref{chap:results_and_discussion} speaks to each part of this gap on the dataset and judge model studied here: selective keyframe extraction plus a decision prompt that treats DOM-mutation activity as an interpretive cue reduces the false-positive rate on normal interaction video to zero, while more than doubling anomaly recall relative to uniform sampling and using fewer image tokens per clip in the process. At the same time, the study also shows that adding structural evidence \emph{at the selection stage}, rather than only in the prompt, did not reproduce this benefit and in one tested configuration undid part of it. This qualifies rather than overturns the general claim that structural evidence is useful: it appears to matter which stage of the pipeline the DOM signal is used at, a distinction the pre-registered ablation was specifically designed to detect, and did detect.

\section{Limitations Recap}
Chapter~\ref{chap:results_and_discussion} (Section~\ref{sec:results-limitations}) sets out the study's limitations in full. In summary, the headline comparison against the naive baseline is aliased across two design factors and cannot be decomposed with the cells that were run; the finding that DOM fusion harms accuracy is established only under the dual-path prompt and not corroborated under the naive prompt; one defect category (\texttt{displaynone\_collapse}) is detected in only 2 of 40 clip-condition pairs; all results derive from a single pinned judge model and a single, synthetically generated reference application; and the scoring pipeline currently reports full bootstrap dispersion for accuracy only, not yet for every metric the project's own reporting rule requires. None of this is treated in this dissertation as invalidating the headline result. Each point defines a specific, scoped extension rather than an open-ended one, and these are set out below.

\section{Future Work}
Six specific pieces of follow-up work follow directly from the limitations above. The first and most immediate is to de-alias the headline contrast by running the A1B0 cell (pixel-only selection, naive prompt) as a new, separately pre-registered experiment, so the accuracy gain currently attributed jointly to selection and prompting in Finding 3 can be attributed to each factor individually. That same cell would also let the fusion-harms-accuracy finding be tested under the naive prompt, by scoring the already-collected A3B0 responses against it, corroborating Finding 2 outside the single prompt condition it currently depends on.

A second and related piece of work is to explain the fusion reversal at the frame level, by directly comparing which frames A1 and A3 select on the 8 clips where they disagree under the dual-path prompt (Finding 2). This is needed to establish a verified mechanism for why down-weighting DOM-accompanied transitions, intended to favour CSS-only defects such as \texttt{flicker\_seizure}, instead loses the most ground on exactly that category.

The \texttt{displaynone\_collapse} blind spot is a further concrete target, most likely addressed by refining the dual-path prompt's decision rule or by adding a structural signal that distinguishes a section being removed from a section's contents being edited, since the current DOM-mutation-proximity heuristic conflates the two. Beyond the pipeline's own design, two extensions would test how far the results generalise: evaluating a second judge model, in particular the open-weight Qwen2.5-VL comparison originally scoped for this project (Section~\ref{sec:env_setup-model}), and extending the fault-injection methodology to other UI frameworks and, where feasible, to naturally occurring defects with independent human verification of ground truth (Section~\ref{sec:results-feedback}), to establish external validity beyond the single TodoMVC instance studied here.

Finally, two disclosed tooling and reporting gaps should be closed: restoring \texttt{statsmodels} to the analysis environment where it is available, and extending the scoring script to emit bootstrap dispersion for false-positive rate, recall, and defect-type match alongside accuracy, so every metric in future reporting is traceable in the machine-readable summary without ad hoc recomputation.

\section{Closing Remarks}
This dissertation set out to test whether combining selective video input construction with structural DOM evidence could improve zero-shot VLM detection of temporal web UI defects beyond both the pixel-level oracle and naive uniform-sampling baselines. The pre-registered, statistically powered evaluation supports that claim for the specific architecture this dissertation ultimately proposes, pixel-driven keyframe selection paired with a DOM-aware decision prompt, while also disproving, rather than quietly dropping, the more elaborate fused-selection design originally intended as the full method. Reporting a negative, pre-registered result alongside a positive one is treated here as part of the contribution, not a shortfall of it: the ablation was designed specifically to be able to tell the two apart, and it did.
