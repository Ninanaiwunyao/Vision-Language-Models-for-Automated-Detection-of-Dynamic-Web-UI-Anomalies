\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}


\section{Summary of the Work}
\label{sec:conclusion-summary}

This dissertation addressed the detection of dynamic anomalies in web user
interfaces, a class of defect that neither functional assertion nor
conventional visual regression testing reliably captures. Functional
frameworks assert over structural state and therefore pass whenever that
state eventually converges, irrespective of the visual trajectory towards
it. Visual regression testing observes appearance but does so at isolated
instants and with a pixel-level oracle whose false-positive rate has been
measured above \SI{93}{\percent} on non-meaningful
changes~\citep{zhang2026beyond}.

The position taken here was that applying a vision-language model to this
problem naively reproduces the difficulty rather than resolving it, and
that the productive intervention lies in the construction of the evidence
the model receives. Two components were developed accordingly: a keyframe
selector that concentrates the model's limited input budget on moments of
change, and a dual-path decision procedure that fuses the model's semantic
judgement with structural evidence rather than accepting the judgement
directly.

These components were evaluated on 120 recordings generated by automated
fault injection, under four conditions with three repetitions, totalling
1{,}440 model invocations. The results were mixed, and are summarised
below in the order of the research questions.


\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:answers}

\paragraph{RQ1: does keyframe selection improve accuracy?}
No. The selector-only contrast between uniform sampling and fused
selection was null: accuracy 0.753 against 0.756, a discordant split of 11
against 12, $p_{\text{Holm}} = 1.000$, Cohen's $g = 0.022$. Substituting
adaptive selection for uniform sampling does not by itself make the model
more accurate. Its value lies elsewhere, in resource consumption.

\paragraph{RQ2: does the dual-path decision procedure reduce false
positives?}
Yes, and completely. With the selector held constant, false positive rate
fell from 0.117 to exactly 0.000, in every one of the three runs, with all
seven false positives corrected and none introduced. The same result was
obtained under the other selector, where the rate fell from 0.167 to
0.000. Critically, this suppression was not purchased with sensitivity:
recall rose rather than fell. On accuracy the same contrast did not
survive multiple-comparison correction ($p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.130$,
$g = 0.208$), and this is reported as obtained.

\paragraph{RQ3: does fusing the structural signal into selection help?}
No; it harms. With the prompt held constant, adding DOM mutation events to
the selection criterion reduced accuracy from 0.908 to 0.842. All eight
discordant clips favoured pixel-only selection and none favoured fusion,
$p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.031$, $g = 0.500$ --- the largest effect size
observed in the study. The fused selector, which this work originally set
out to validate, is therefore falsified.

\paragraph{RQ4: what does each condition cost?}
Adaptive selection reduced token consumption by between 39 and 49 per cent
relative to the baseline, significant at $p_{\text{Holm}} < 10^{-6}$ with
a rank-biserial correlation of 1.00, indicating the reduction held for
every clip. The saving is attributable to reducing the frame count from
six to three, not to fusion: the fused selector consumed marginally more
tokens than pixel-only selection is estimated to require. Total inference
cost remained below \num{0.0011} US dollars per recording throughout.

\section{Contributions Revisited}
\label{sec:contributions-revisited}

Three findings constitute the substantive contribution of this work, two
positive and one negative.

First, the dual-path decision procedure eliminated false positives
entirely, under both selectors and in every repetition, while raising
anomaly recall. This is the component that worked, and it is a
prompting-and-fusion intervention requiring no model training, no
fine-tuning and approximately 676 additional input tokens per recording.

Second, adaptive keyframe selection was shown to be a cost intervention
rather than an accuracy intervention. This is a null result on accuracy
and a strong positive result on resources, and the separation of the two
is itself informative: it indicates that the frames a uniform sampler
misses are not, in this setting, the frames that determine the verdict.

Third, and contrary to the design hypothesis, incorporating structural
signals into keyframe selection degraded performance. Taken together with
the success of the dual-path procedure, which also consumes structural
evidence, the results support a distinction that was not anticipated at
the outset: structural evidence is valuable at the point of decision,
where it can be weighed against a semantic judgement, and harmful at the
point of selection, where it competes with the visual signal for a fixed
frame budget. Structural evidence answers whether an observed change was
legitimate; it does not answer when to look.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The proposed approach has a number of limitations, set out here in the
interest of an accurate account of what has and has not been established.

\paragraph{The aliased headline contrast.}
The largest improvement observed, from the baseline to the proposed
method, changes both experimental factors simultaneously. The cell that
would resolve this aliasing, A1B0, was not executed, and was deliberately
not added after the results were known in order to preserve the
pre-specified design. Consequently the evidence that fusion harms the
method rests solely on comparisons made under the dual-path prompt and
remains uncorroborated under the naive prompt. A partial de-aliasing
argument is available from the null selector-only contrast, but it assumes
that a finding obtained for the fused selector generalises to the
pixel-only selector, which was not tested.

\paragraph{Synthetic data and external validity.}
The evaluation was conducted entirely on recordings generated by injecting
faults into a controlled reference application served locally. This design
affords exact ground truth and reproducible defect timing, both necessary
for the paired comparisons undertaken, but it does not establish that the
findings transfer to production interfaces. Real applications exhibit
network variability, third-party content and framework behaviours that the
controlled setting excludes. Generalisation to recordings drawn from
real-world corpora was not tested.

\paragraph{A single application and a single model.}
All recordings derive from one reference application, and all conditions
used one model at temperature zero. Interface conventions, component
density and animation style vary considerably across real applications,
and the extent to which the selection thresholds transfer to different
visual styles was not examined. Nor is it known whether the components
would yield comparable improvements with a model of different capability,
or whether a more capable model would render them unnecessary.

\paragraph{Defect taxonomy coverage and a persistent blind spot.}
The six injected defect classes were chosen to be programmatically
reproducible and visually manifest within a short recording. Defects
developing over longer sessions, depending on backend state, or visually
subtle by nature are not represented. Moreover, one of the six classes,
\texttt{displaynone\_collapse}, was detected in only 2 of 40
clip-conditions and remained a blind spot under every condition including
the proposed method. The failure analysis indicates that the method is
weakest precisely where a defect produces a state the application could
plausibly have reached correctly, and the taxonomy does not systematically
sample that difficult region.

\paragraph{Defect characterisation.}
Among correctly detected anomalies, the predicted defect class matched
ground truth in only 31 of 49 cases under the proposed method. Correct
detection therefore does not imply correct characterisation, and the
practical value of the \texttt{bug\_type} field for triage is
correspondingly limited.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:futurework}

Several directions follow directly from the findings and limitations
above.

\paragraph{Resolving the aliasing.}
Executing the omitted A1B0 cell as a separate, appropriately declared
follow-up study would decompose the headline improvement cleanly and
establish whether the harm caused by fusion also obtains under the naive
prompt. This is the single most valuable extension, and its exclusion here
was a matter of experimental discipline rather than of interest.

\paragraph{Structural signals used correctly.}
The central finding of this work is that structural evidence helps at
decision time and hurts at selection time. That distinction invites direct
investigation: a selector that uses only the visual signal, paired with a
decision layer given progressively richer structural context, would test
whether the benefit of structural evidence scales with its quality at the
point where it demonstrably helps.

\paragraph{A more discriminating structural signal.}
The persistent blind spot arises because a removed content block resembles
legitimate emptiness, particularly when unrelated DOM activity nearby is
read as user-initiated change. A structural signal that distinguishes
element removal not preceded by a corresponding user input event would
address this case directly, and could be evaluated on the existing dataset
without new recordings.

\paragraph{Cross-model evaluation.}
Holding the model constant isolated the contribution of the components but
left open whether those contributions are model-dependent. Repeating the
design across models of differing capability, including open-weight models
runnable locally, would establish whether the benefit persists and whether
it diminishes as model capability increases.

\paragraph{Validation on real-world recordings.}
Applying the pipeline to a corpus of real GUI interaction recordings would
test external validity. This requires resolving the oracle problem for
data whose ground truth is not known by construction, for which two
approaches are available: comparison against a known-good reference
recording of the same flow, or evaluation against a general anomaly
checklist rather than a specified expectation.


\section{Concluding Remarks}
\label{sec:concluding}

This work set out to show that a fused visual and structural keyframe
selector would improve dynamic web UI anomaly detection. It did not. What
the experiment showed instead is narrower but firmer: that the decisive
intervention is not what the model is shown but what it is asked, and that
the same structural evidence which degrades performance when used to
choose frames eliminates false positives entirely when used to ground a
verdict.

That result is less convenient than the one anticipated, but it is more
useful. It identifies where effort should be directed in a setting where
inference budget is finite and false alarms, rather than missed detections,
are what cause automated visual testing to be abandoned in practice.
